Theoretical Perspective
Thomas Kuhn's (1962) concept of a 
paradigm shift
 in 
The Structure of Scientific Revolutions 
involves a fundamental change in the 
basic concepts
 and the 
experimental practices 
of a scientific discipline. 
Such shifts are often characterized by the displacement of an existing framework with a new one that is incompatible with the old. Examples include the shift from Newtonian mechanics to Einstein's theory of relativity, or the transition from the phlogiston theory of combustion to the chemical theory of oxidation.
In contrast, a 
Gestalt shift
 refers to a change in perception, that allows one to see a different aspect of an object or situation without altering the object itself. 
It is akin to viewing an image in a new way, such as the famous "duck-rabbit" illusion, where one can switch between seeing a duck and a rabbit, without the image itself changing. Or the Necker cube illusion.
Evolutionary Culturology: Not a `True' Kuhnian Paradigm-Shift-? (...Or 
IS

This new framework does not negate or invalidate the principles and findings of existing sciences, but rather 
integrates them

While Ev Cult exhibits

3. Law 3 (Downward Command/Control): Larger systems command and control smaller subsystems. In geology, mountain ranges consist of individual mountains, rocks, and minerals. In information science, data packets flow within nodes and connections of digital networks.
