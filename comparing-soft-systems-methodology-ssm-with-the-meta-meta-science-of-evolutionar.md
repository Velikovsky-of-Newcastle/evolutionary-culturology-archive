Abstract
This paper compares and contrasts Peter Checkland's Soft
Systems Methodology (SSM) (1990) with Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult) (2016), focusing on
their isomorphies and differences. Both methodologies aim to address complex
problems through structured approaches but differ significantly in their scope,
application, and underlying philosophies. While SSM is designed for addressing
human-centric, organizational problems, Ev Cult offers a universal framework
applicable across all domains of knowledge. This paper explores these
methodologies in detail, highlighting how they can inform and enrich each
other.

A Brief Summary of Peter Checkland's Soft Systems
Methodology (SSM)
Peter Checkland's Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) (1990) is a
qualitative approach used to address complex, ill-structured problems, often
referred to as "soft problems." Unlike traditional systems
engineering, which is more suited for "hard" problems with
well-defined goals and processes, SSM is designed for situations where there is
uncertainty about the problem itself, and multiple perspectives must be
considered.
Key Concepts and Steps in SSM:
Problem
     Situation
: Recognize and describe the problem situation, acknowledging
     that it is messy and not well-defined. Multiple stakeholders may have
     differing views on what the problem is.
Rich
     Picture
: Develop a "rich picture" to visually represent the
     problem situation, capturing the various elements, interactions, and
     perspectives involved. This helps to understand the complexity and the
     different viewpoints.
Root
     Definitions (RDs)
: Formulate root definitions for relevant systems
     within the problem context. A root definition is a concise description of
     a system's purpose, which includes elements such as:
CATWOE
:
      An acronym standing for Customers, Actors, Transformation process,
      Worldview, Owner, and Environmental constraints. These elements help
      structure the root definition.
Conceptual
     Models
: Create conceptual models of the systems identified in the root
     definitions. These models outline what the system should ideally do
     according to each root definition, without yet considering real-world
     constraints.
Comparison
:
     Compare the conceptual models with the real-world situation. This involves
     looking at where the model aligns or differs from the actual situation and
     considering different stakeholder perspectives.
Feasible,
     Desirable Changes
: Identify changes that are both feasible and
     desirable to improve the situation. These changes should address the
     discrepancies identified in the comparison step.
Action
     to Improve
: Implement the proposed changes, continuously involving
     stakeholders to ensure that the interventions are appropriate and
     beneficial.
Application:
SSM is particularly useful in organizational change, policy
analysis, and any situation where human factors and diverse perspectives play a
critical role. It is an iterative process, meaning that the understanding of
the problem and the proposed solutions evolve as stakeholders engage with the
methodology.
Summary:
SSM is a holistic, flexible framework that emphasizes
understanding different perspectives and iteratively refining both the problem
definition and the solutions. It is well-suited for complex, human-centric
issues where traditional linear problem-solving approaches may fall short.
Brief Summary of Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult)
Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult) is a comprehensive
meta-meta-scientific approach designed to analyze and integrate knowledge
across all domains of reality, including biological, cultural, and
technological systems. Unlike traditional methodologies that focus on specific
disciplines or domains, Ev Cult provides a universal framework that applies to
all systems, enabling the identification and understanding of the fundamental
units of culture and their interactions. This approach is rooted in the concept
of fractal HOLON/partons, which are the structural and functional units that
operate across different scales of reality.
 
Key Concepts and Steps in Ev Cult:
1. Fractal HOLON/partons: Recognize that all systems,
whether biological, cultural, or technological, are composed of fractal
HOLON/partons. These units function at various scale levels, from the smallest
particles to complex systems like societies and ecosystems.
2. Three Laws of Systems and Culture: Apply the three
universal laws governing fractal HOLON/partons: 
   - Law 1:
Integration upwards into larger systems.
   - Law 2:
Cooperation and competition sideways with units on the same scale level.
   - Law 3: Command
and control downwards over smaller units within the system.
3. Universal Taxonomy of Culture: Classify all units of
culture (e.g., ideas, processes, products) into a universal taxonomy,
understanding that these units can exist as mental, physical, or informational
entities.
4. Evolutionary Mechanisms: Use evolutionary algorithms and
mechanisms, such as Selection, Variation, and Transmission (SVT), to understand
how these units of culture change and evolve over time. This approach applies
across all domains, from biological evolution to cultural creativity.
5. Consilience: Seek consilience, or the unity of knowledge,
by integrating insights from all scientific and cultural domains into a
cohesive understanding. This involves using the Ev Cult framework to bridge
gaps between disciplines and create a holistic view of reality.
6. Iterative Integration: Continuously refine and integrate
knowledge across different domains and scale levels, recognizing that
understanding evolves as new data and perspectives are incorporated.
Application:
As a meta-meta-science, Ev Cult applies to all domains. 
As it achieves consilience, the unity of knowledge, the meta-meta-science of Ev Cult is particularly valuable in domains that require an
integrative, interdisciplinary approach, such as cultural evolution, creativity
studies, systems science, and artificial intelligence. 
It is also applicable to
any situation that involves complex systems, and the need for a unified
understanding across multiple domains.
Summary:
Ev Cult is a meta-meta-scientific framework that provides a
universal approach to analyzing and understanding all systems in reality. By
using the concept of fractal HOLON/partons and the three universal laws, it
enables the integration of knowledge across all domains and scale levels,
fostering consilience and a comprehensive understanding of complex systems.
This methodology is iterative and adaptable, making it suitable for both
scientific inquiry and practical applications in diverse domains.
