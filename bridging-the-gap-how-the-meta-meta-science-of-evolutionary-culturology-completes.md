Background to the Problem: The quest for a 
truly General
 Theory of
Systems
The quest for a General Theory of Systems has long been a
central objective within the domain of systems science, dating back to the
foundational work of Ludwig von Bertalanffy and his peers in the 1950s and
1960s (e.g. 
GST
, 1968). 
The need for such a scientific theory stems from the
inherent complexity and interconnectedness of the universe, where systems at
various scales—from the subatomic up to the cosmic—exhibit patterns of behavior
that are consistent yet varied, across different domains. 
The challenge lay in developing a singular unified framework
that could adequately model all these systems, taking into account the diverse
languages, methodologies, and mathematical representations that have evolved in
different domains of study.
In 2018, James N. Rose, Chair of the General Systems
Mathematics and Languaging Special Integration Group within the 
International Society for Systems Sciences (ISSS)
, issued an invitation to members of the
ISSS, to engage in a critical examination of his admirable framing paper, 
Nested
Levels of Organized Systems: A New Model of Multiple Nested Interacting
Entropies That Result in the Production of Complexity 
(2018)
.
 
This paper
represents Rose's attempt to contribute to the ongoing development of a General
Theory of Systems by addressing specific gaps in current scientific
understanding, particularly the disjunctions between different mathematical
models and the languages used to describe complex systems.
Rose's (2018) Call to Action
Rose's insightful invitation underscores several key issues
that have hindered progress toward a General Theory of Systems since the 1950s.
He rightly highlights the importance of recognizing the value of different 
frames of
reference
, urging investigators to appreciate the commonalities between
different scientific disciplines, despite the differences in terminology and
conceptual frameworks. Rose emphasizes that while the Field (the producers and
consumers) for every domain brings its own established perspectives and
knowledge, the success of a General Theory of Systems depends on the ability to
integrate these diverse viewpoints into a single coherent whole.
One of the central challenges Rose rightly identifies is the
underutilization of mathematics within the ISSS, and the broader Systems Science
community. While mathematics has historically been a cornerstone of scientific
inquiry, its role in Systems Science has diminished over recent decades,
particularly as fields like 
Chaos Theory
, Complexity Science, and 
Cybernetics
have gained prominence. Rose argues that this trend is problematic because
mathematics - as a universal language - is crucial for developing and
communicating scientific ideas across cultures and disciplines. He calls for a
revival of mathematical modeling within Systems Science, advocating for the
development of new mathematical frameworks that can better capture the
complexities of the universe.
The Role of the 
General Systems Mathematics and
Languaging SIG
The establishment of the "General Systems Mathematics
and Languaging SIG" represents a renewed commitment to these objectives
within the ISSS. Rose envisions this SIG as a forum for rigorous scientific
inquiry, where mathematical properties and relations are analyzed alongside
natural vernacular language descriptions of systems. 
The SIG's mission aligns with the original objectives of the
ISSS, which include promoting the unity of science, through improved
communication among specialists, facilitating the transfer of concepts and
models across disciplines, and encouraging the development of new theoretical
models where they are lacking (see: 
https://www.isss.org/history/
and 
https://www.isss.org/by-laws/
).
Rose’s invitation to the SIG participants is more than just
a call to read and discuss his paper; it is a call to action for the Systems Science community to re-engage with the foundational principles of their domain.
He emphasizes the need for an "encyclopedic reference frame and
scope" that can address the full spectrum of systems, from the Big Bang, through
to the most complex phenomena observed by science. This ambitious agenda
reflects the aspirations of the ISSS founders to discover the "absolute
shared qualia of a coherent, consistent, co-dependent, compatibly functioning
Universe."

A Brief Summary of Rose (2018): `
Nested Levels of
Organized Systems: A New Model of Multiple Nested Interacting Entropies That
Result in the Production of Complexity'
James N. Rose's (2018) article proposes a new holistic model for
understanding the organization of the universe through 
nested interacting
entropies
. Rose argues that current mathematical paradigms are insufficient
to explain the complexity of the universe due to foundational
misunderstandings, especially between different scientific domains like
relativity, and quantum mechanics.
Rose introduces a model that integrates Prigogine's
statistical emergence of complexity, with Mandelbrot's fractal
(non-statistical) emergence of complexity. He critiques existing mathematical
frameworks for lacking the internal definitions necessary to correlate and
explain all dynamics in the universe coherently. The proposed model aims to
offer new mathematical relations that bridge these gaps, suggesting that
complexity emerges from nested levels of interacting entropies, leading to new
self-stable complex organizations.
While Rose’s approach is innovative, and addresses
significant gaps in current understanding, it also (currently) faces challenges in
conceptualization, empirical validation, and its ability to apply universally
across all domains.
