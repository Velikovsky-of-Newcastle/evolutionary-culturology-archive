The Two Cultures become One: Evolutionary Culturology’s Solution to Consilience, the unity of all knowledge
For decades, scholars have recognized the deep divide between the Sciences and the Arts/Humanities. 
In his famous 1959 Rede Lecture, C.P. Snow lamented the growing rift between "the two cultures"—the sciences, and the literary intellectuals—arguing that this schism had led to a fragmented intellectual landscape, where each domain was ignorant of the other (Snow, 1959/1998). 
Snow saw this division as an impediment to solving the world's greatest challenges, as it prevented interdisciplinary collaboration, and the synthesis of knowledge.
Nearly 40 years later, E.O. Wilson echoed Snow’s concerns, in 
Consilience: The Unity of Knowledge 
(1998). 
Wilson argued that the sciences and humanities must be reintegrated under a single, cohesive framework, in order to achieve true intellectual synthesis. However, while 
Consilience
 proposed the goal of unification, it lacked a formal structural mechanism to achieve it (E O Wilson, 1998).
 
Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult) provides the missing framework that neither Snow nor Wilson could fully articulate. By demonstrating that all knowledge domains—scientific, artistic, technological, and philosophical—follow the same 
fractal HOLON/parton 
structure, function (behavior), and the same three universal laws, Ev Cult dissolves the artificial boundary between the sciences and the humanities. 
It reveals that knowledge itself is a singular, unified, evolving system, rather than a series of isolated disciplines, or, as D S Wilson dubs it, an “ivory archipelago”.
Other scholars have similarly sought to bridge this disciplinary divide, by applying evolutionary principles to cultural and artistic domains. Joseph Carroll, in 
Evolution and Literary Theory 
(1995), argued that literary criticism should be grounded in evolutionary biology, positioning storytelling within a framework of adaptation and selection. Brian Boyd extended this perspective in 
On the Origin of Stories: Evolution, Cognition, and Fiction 
(2009), emphasizing how evolutionary theory can explain narrative structures and the cognitive appeal of fiction. More recently, David Sloan Wilson’s 
This View of Life: Completing the Darwinian Revolution 
(2019) applied evolutionary insights to human culture more broadly, advocating for a comprehensive framework that integrates scientific and social systems.
Through the Ev Cult lens, the Two Cultures problem is not merely a sociological divide, but a simple misunderstanding of how knowledge functions... 
The Sciences and Arts/Humanities are not separate entities; they are manifestations of the same underlying structural principles. 
Ev Cult resolves this by providing a systematic, formalized approach to consilience—one that is not just philosophical but scientifically demonstrable. 
Where Snow identified the problem and E O Wilson and others envisioned the solution, Evolutionary Culturology delivers the scientific mechanism that makes that solution a reality.
