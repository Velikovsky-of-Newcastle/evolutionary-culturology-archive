Brief Intellectual Biography of Velikovsky of Newcastle
In 1986, as a Year 11 student in Taree, New South Wales, Velikovsky’s intellectual journey began in earnest with his reading of Lyall Watson’s 
Lifetide
. 
This book, which explored evolution and culture, introduced Velikovsky to Karl Popper’s 
Three Worlds
 theory, marking the start of his fascination with evolutionary theory, though he did not consciously realize Popper’s significance at the time. 
Alongside Watson, Velikovsky was deeply influenced by Charles Darwin’s evolutionary ideas, particularly through 
On the Origin of Species
, and by Joseph Heller’s satirical novel 
Catch-22
, which inspired his own early comedy-satire fiction writing.
The following year, as he completed his Higher School Certificate, Velikovsky deepened his engagement with Darwin and Popper, while also continuing to reflect on cultural evolution and creativity. He would later write the short story 
Popper and Darwin’s Dinner Conversation
, illustrating the early influence of these thinkers on his creative and philosophical outlook.
In 1988, after high school, Velikovsky worked in various trades, including farming and construction. During this period, he read James Gleick’s 
Chaos: Making a New Science
, which fueled his interest in chaos theory, and scientific paradigm shifts. His exploration of Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi’s systems model of creativity also laid the foundation for his later work in understanding cultural production as a system of inputs, processes, and outputs.
By 1990, Velikovsky’s intellectual and creative pursuits began to coalesce around songwriting, filmmaking, videogames, TV, and communication studies. He devoured texts like Stephen Citron’s 
Song Writing
 and John Fiske’s 
Introduction to Communication Studies
, which shaped his understanding of media and creativity. During this year, he also wrote 365 songs—one for every day of the year—demonstrating his prolific creative output. He went on to play in various bands (Texas Radio, The 3-Toned Sloths, The Midnight Concreters, and many more.)
In 1991, Velikovsky formally entered academia, enrolling in a Bachelor of Communication program at the University of Newcastle. There, he studied foundational works such as Claude Shannon’s 
The Mathematical Theory of Communication
, Norbert Wiener’s 
Cybernetics
, and David Brown’s 
Human Universals
. This period marked the beginning of his formal engagement with systems theory, cybernetics, and the universality of human culture.
A pivotal moment occurred in 1992, when Velikovsky discovered the universal structure and function of the unit of culture—the 
fractal HOLON/parton
. 
Inspired by his exposure to Dawkins’ 
The Selfish Gene
, a documentary on fractal geometry, and Arthur Koestler’s 
The Act of Creation
, as well as Feynman (1975) on partons, Velikovsky discovered that all cultural units shared a fractal structure. 
However, he chose to keep this discovery private for the next 20 years, rigorously testing it in industry, across over 50 domains, before formally publishing on it, in his 2016 PhD on Evolutionary Culturology.
Velikovsky’s discovery of the 
fractal HOLON/parton
 structure was directly influenced by Popper’s 
Three Worlds
 theory, which he had first encountered in 
Lifetide
. 
In Popper’s framework, World 1 encompassed physical objects, World 2 mental states, and World 3 abstract ideas. Velikovsky extended this model by adding a fourth category—physical processes—creating a comprehensive framework for understanding the structure of all culture. This intellectual breakthrough would become the cornerstone of his doctoral research and one of the foundations of the meta-meta-science of Evolutionary Culturology.
Throughout the 1990s, Velikovsky’s intellectual journey was defined by a dual focus on theory and practice. He worked in media production, writing for television, theatre, and film, while also continuing his academic studies. In 1995, after reading over 100 books on screenwriting and narratology, he published 
The Feature Film Screenwriter’s Workbook
, which summarized the tools and techniques of the craft. His creative output during this period included writing and directing short films and collaborating on screenplays and game scripts.
As Velikovsky’s career progressed, he moved into game design and consulting, applying his evolving theories of culture and creativity to the world of interactive media. In 1998, Velikovsky read E.O. Wilson’s 
Consilience: The Unity of Knowledge
, which reinforced his belief in Ev Cult as a unified theory of culture. 
Popper’s philosophy of problem-solving continued to inform his thinking, particularly in how Velikovsky approached complex creative and intellectual challenges.
In the early 2000s, Velikovsky’s exploration of evolutionary psychology and group creativity led him to further refine his fractal HOLON/parton theory. His work in game design and writing was increasingly informed by his understanding of evolutionary mechanisms, creativity, and cultural production. During this period, he also explored the relationship between culture and cognition, engaging with texts such as Boyd’s 
On the Origin of Stories
 and Pinker’s 
How the Mind Works
.
In 2012, Velikovsky began his PhD in Evolutionary Culturology at the University of Newcastle. His research empirically studied units of culture using Ev Cult, with a focus on cinema and narrative fiction. 
Popper’s problem-solution schema played a central role in Velikovsky’s Ev Cult PhD methodology, guiding his efforts to present his solution to the scientific problem of the universal structure and function of all cultural units. 
The culmination of his research was the formalization of the 
fractal HOLON/parton 
theory, which provides a universal framework for understanding culture (information: ideas, processes, products) across all domains.
By 2016, Velikovsky had completed his PhD, establishing Evolutionary Culturology as a meta-meta-science—a comprehensive framework that bridges the arts, sciences, and social sciences. 
Popper’s 
Three Worlds
 theory remained an essential lens in Velikovsky’s work, helping him categorize and analyze cultural units with precision. His doctoral dissertation was groundbreaking, offering a solution to the long-standing problem of consilience—the unity of knowledge.
Following the completion of his PhD, Velikovsky continued to apply his theories in consulting work across multiple domains, from film and media to artificial intelligence and transdisciplinary research. His consulting work, grounded in 
fractal HOLON/parton 
theory, emphasizes the practical applications of Evolutionary Culturology in solving complex problems in science, technology, and creativity.
In recent years, Velikovsky was focused on developing
