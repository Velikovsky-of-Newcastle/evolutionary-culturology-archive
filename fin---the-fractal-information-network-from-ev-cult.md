Introduction
Standard Knowledge Graphs use: source nodes, relational vectors, and target nodes. 
For example, the text “Marie Curie won the Nobel Prize”, when converted to a standard KG, becomes: (source node) “Marie Curie”; and a relational arrow (edge) from that node, pointing to (target node) “Nobel Prize”, and the arrow (or the `edge’, in a directed graph) is labelled “WON”. 
Knowledge Graphs (KGs) are widely used for organizing and analyzing data, including when combined with LLMs - but traditional designs often fail to capture the (1) hierarchical, (2) cooperative, competitive, and (3) command/control dynamics, inherent to all real-world systems in all domains.

Rooted in Ev Cult’s three universal laws, FIN represents systems as fractal HOLON/partons, enabling a nuanced, multi-layered approach to knowledge representation. This capability is central to The EthiSizer AI, which uses FIN to simulate the Whole Earth, fostering super-ethical cooperative solutions for global challenges.
Principles and Design of the FIN
The FIN introduces several novel features:

Hierarchical Node Placement
Nodes are vertically arranged to reflect systemic control (Law #3: Command/Control Downwards) and laterally placed to indicate cooperation or competition (Law #2a/b: Cooperate/Compete Sideways). For instance, "Marie Curie" integrates upwards into the "Nobel Prize in Physics 1903," while collaborating with "Pierre Curie" and competing with "Other Nominees" (Figure 1).

Four Relationship Types

Cooperates With (Law #2a): Collaboration among peers.
o
 
Competes With (Law #2b): Competition among peers.
o
 
Commands/Controls (Law #3): Influence exerted by higher nodes over lower ones.

Multi-Layered Structure
FIN operates as an intermediary between the Ev Cult Universal Domain Map (UDM) and domain-specific KGs. This layered design situates detailed domain knowledge within broader systemic contexts, enabling insights into global interdependencies.
