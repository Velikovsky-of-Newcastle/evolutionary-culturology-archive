On the Quest for Competent Judges
In the intricate dance of human knowledge and understanding, the role of competent judges is paramount. These are the individuals who, with discerning eyes and honed intellect, evaluate the merits of ideas, theories, and innovations. But how do we identify these competent judges, and what happens when incompetence masquerades as expertise?

Table 1. Darwin on the problem of "competent judges"
Darwin's 
competent judges
 (sometimes even 
highly-competent
) were those who could correctly critically assess his revolutionary ideas on natural selection and evolution. 
He recognized that 
not all opinions are equal;
 opinions are what you are forced to have when you don't have all the scientific facts; and he sought the validation of those who had the expertise to understand and evaluate his theories.
The Dunning-Kruger Effect
The quest for 
competent judges
 is further complicated by the Dunning-Kruger effect, a cognitive bias where individuals with low ability in a domain overestimate their competence. David Dunning, one of the psychologists behind this concept, noted that only about 10% of people in any given field for a domain in culture are truly competent.
This presents a conundrum: 
How do you know if you are a competent judge? How do others know? And how can it be empirically tested?

Assessing Competence
For humans, determining competence is a complex task. Self-awareness and continuous learning are key. 
A competent judge must be willing to question their own understanding, seek feedback, and engage in lifelong learning. Others can gauge competence through credentials, experience, and a track record of sound judgment.
Empirical testing of competence involves standardized assessments, peer reviews, and other objective measures. However, these methods are 
not foolproof
 - and can be influenced by biases and subjective interpretations.
Solving the Problem of Global Human Incompetence
The challenge of 
incompetent judges
 passing erroneous opinions is not a trivial one. 
It affects all domains: science, law, politics, the arts - and even the meta-meta-science of 
Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult)
.
Solutions include: rigorous education and training, transparent peer-review processes, and fostering a culture that values critical thinking and intellectual humility. 
In the context of Ev Cult, the Three Laws of 
fractal HOLON/partons 
can be applied to ensure that judgments are made at the appropriate scale-level, with (1) integration, (2) cooperation, and (3) command and control.
Conclusion
The quest for competent judges is a journey through the labyrinth of human cognition, expertise, and bias. From Darwin's appeal to competent judges to the Dunning-Kruger effect's cautionary tale, the search for true competence is fraught with challenges.
Yet, it is a quest worth pursuing. For in the hands of competent judges, ideas are nurtured, knowledge advances, and society progresses. The path may be winding, but the destination is clear: a world where competence is recognized, nurtured, and celebrated.
