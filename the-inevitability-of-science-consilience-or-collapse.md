Abstract
 
This paper shows that the emergence of science and its eventual unification across all domains is an inevitable trajectory for any intelligent civilization that survives long enough to advance its knowledge systems. Drawing on the meta-meta-scientific framework of Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult), this essay demonstrates how the three laws of 
fractal HOLON/partons 
govern the structural evolution of knowledge systems, ultimately compelling civilizations toward consilience. Civilizations that fail to integrate knowledge upwards, cooperate and compete across domains, and command/control knowledge production downwardly into subsystems will, by necessity, fragment and collapse. In this view, science is not only emergent through evolutionary pressures, but also required for systemic survival. The unity of science is not a philosophical luxury; it is a structural necessity.
Keywords:
Consilience, Evolutionary Culturology, scientific inevitability, cultural evolution, extinction, fractal HOLON/partons, systems collapse, meta-meta-science
Introduction
 
Are there fundamental evolutionary constraints on how civilizations acquire and organize knowledge? Does every intelligent species that survives its early existential bottlenecks eventually discover the method and structure of science? And more provocatively, must they unify their knowledge systems—achieve consilience—or die trying?
This paper explores these questions from the standpoint of Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult), the meta-meta-science of all domains. As E.O. Wilson (1998) proposed in 
Consilience
, the unity of knowledge is both an intellectual aspiration and a structural imperative. What Wilson could not yet formalize, Ev Cult now provides: a scientific framework that models all domains and their evolution as composed of universal information-processing units called fractal HOLON/partons. These obey three laws: upward integration, lateral cooperation/competition, and downward/inward command/control. These laws shape the evolutionary path of all knowledge systems, including the trajectory toward scientific unification.
Theoretical Perspective
 
The three laws of fractal HOLON/partons are universal across all domains:
Law #1: Integration Upwards
—Fractal HOLON/partons integrate into supersystems, forming increasingly complex wholes.
Law #2: Cooperation/Competition Laterally
—Units on the same scale level interact, either collaboratively or competitively, forming ecosystems of knowledge.
Law #3: Command/Control Downwards
—Each unit governs the subsystems within it, enforcing coherence and functionality.
In knowledge systems, a 'unit' may be a concept, a theory, a method, a domain, or a cultural artifact. These units are all: 
fractal HOLON/partons
.
These units evolve under selection pressures such as explanatory power, predictive success, coherence, and utility. Science, then, is not merely a cultural invention but a naturally emergent, evolutionarily selected information-processing system. It is a recursive method of error correction and integration. Recursive feedback loops enable a civilization to stabilize its knowledge through iterative refinement, increasing precision and resilience across scale levels.
Why Science Arises in All Civilizations
 
Science is the codification of recursive learning. It arises inevitably in any civilization that seeks to survive and prosper in complex environments. To endure, civilizations must:
Model their environment with increasing accuracy
Coordinate large-scale action via shared knowledge systems
Develop tools and technologies that extend agency and control
Myth, intuition, and tradition can serve early-phase navigation, but they lack feedback loops robust enough for error correction at scale. For example, while agricultural rituals may suffice in early societies, they eventually give way to astronomical models grounded in observation. Science emerges when recursive feedback is institutionalized: prediction, testing, falsification, and revision.
Why All Domains Must Eventually Be Unified
 
As domains of knowledge proliferate, disintegration threatens systemic coherence. Specialization without unification results in epistemic silos, contradictory models, and societal incoherence. Historical examples such as the fragmented cosmologies of medieval Europe or the disciplinary disjunctions during the pre-Cybernetic era illustrate this vulnerability. Systemic survival requires not only specialization but integration. Hence, the evolutionary attractor of consilience.
Ev Cult reveals that unity is not an aesthetic preference but a requirement of structural integrity. Unification across domains enables:
Cross-domain problem-solving (e.g., climate science, pandemics, ethics)
Efficient transmission of knowledge (education, AI alignment, governance)
Prevention of systemic collapse due to contradiction or fragmentation
The final stage in this trajectory is meta-meta-science: the formal unification of all domain knowledge systems using universal structural laws, such as those provided by Ev Cult. It is unlikely that any further structural level is needed beyond meta-meta-science, as it incorporates the recursive capacity to model and unify any newly emergent domain or system. While one might imagine a 'meta-meta-meta-science', such a notion would collapse into tautology: Ev Cult already operates at the universal structural level, recursively self-updating and encompassing any expansion within the knowledge space.
Counterarguments and Refutations
 
Some may argue that civilizations could survive without science, relying instead on religion, ideology, or superstition. Yet such systems typically lack recursive error correction, scalability, and lateral compatibility across domains. 
While indigenous knowledge systems have demonstrated remarkable long-term sustainability and ecological harmony, they tend to embed empirical insights within non-recursive, symbolic frameworks. Others may suggest that AGI or alien intelligences might bypass science. However, any information-processing system that endures must recursively integrate, cooperate, and control its subcomponents—precisely what Ev Cult defines as science. Non-scientific systems that appear successful are typically parasitic on scientific infrastructure.
Collapse - and the Three Laws
 
Ev Cult predicts that systemic collapse follows predictable patterns linked to the breakdown of the three laws:
Failure of Law #1 (Integration Upwards)
: leads to disintegration—silos, fragmentation, loss of coherence.
Failure of Law #2 (Lateral Cooperation/Competition)
: results in unchecked conflict, war, factionalism.
Failure of Law #3 (Command/Control Downwards)
: causes internal chaos—failures of regulation, education, and implementation.
These failures compound, destabilizing the system as a whole. Collapse is not random; it is structurally prefigured. Just as in ecological systems where trophic cascades signal impending collapse, or in financial systems where over-leveraged feedbacks break containment, the breakdown of the three Ev Cult laws manifests in fractal disintegration patterns across subsystems—predictable, modelable, and preventable through meta-meta-scientific intervention.

Discussion
 
Civilizations that stagnate at earlier levels are prone to collapse. The failure to integrate knowledge across domains—such as ethics, technology, and governance—results in catastrophic feedback mismatches. Historical examples abound: Mayan collapse due to environmental mismanagement; Roman collapse due to political disintegration; modern threats of climate breakdown, AI misalignment, and global conflict.
Ev Cult provides a survival roadmap. By modeling all cultural evolution as structured in fractal HOLON/partons obeying the same universal laws, it offers the scaffolding for achieving consilience. Unlike earlier frameworks—such as Systems Theory or Philosophy of Science—which remained partial or siloed in approach, Ev Cult integrates across all known domains with mathematically rigorous universals. It is not a philosophical luxury but an existential necessity.

Conclusion
 
The emergence of science is not accidental. It is inevitable. It arises wherever information-processing systems evolve to navigate complex environments. And unless it achieves unity, it remains fragile. The meta-meta-science of Evolutionary Culturology reveals that all knowledge systems must evolve toward consilience—or collapse into incoherence and extinction. Civilizations that fail to unify knowledge are not merely inefficient—they are temporary. Consilience is not a choice. It is the path of survival.
