Introduction

Theoretical Perspective: The Flaws in the Meme Analogy
The mistaken analogy between genes and memes lies at the heart of memetics. 
Dawkins mistakenly proposed that memes, like genes, are "self-replicating units" subject to variation, selection, and transmission. 
However, this analogy breaks down under scrutiny:
1. Genes Do Not Self-Replicate  
   Even in biology, genes do not "self-replicate." Replication occurs within the context of larger systems—specifically, cells, which provide the machinery necessary for gene duplication. The gene/meme analogy assumes autonomy where none exists. If genes themselves require a systemic context for replication, cultural units—far more complex and embedded in sociocultural systems—cannot plausibly be autonomous "self-replicators."
2. Culture Is Not a Biological Process  
   Memetics erroneously transposes biological principles onto culture, failing to account for the unique dynamics of sociocultural systems. Culture is not a simplistic analog to biology; it is a multi-level system governed by the actions of intentional agents (e.g., humans, AI tools) operating within broader ecosystems.
3. Units of Culture Are Systems, Not Self-Replicators  
   Ev Cult demonstrates that all units of culture—letters, words, sentences, books, songs, movies, philosophies, technologies, and more—are fractal HOLON/partons. These are systems embedded within larger systems, dependent on those systems for their creation, replication, and survival. They are not autonomous entities capable of independent replication.
 
Why Memetics Is Scientifically Invalid
The failures of memetics as a scientific theory can be summarized as follows:
1. No Empirical Validation  
   Despite decades of discourse, there is no evidence that cultural units replicate themselves autonomously or behave like biological genes. Memetics lacks experimental support and operational definitions necessary for scientific investigation.
2. Lack of Mechanism  
   Memetics assumes that cultural units act as "self-replicating entities," but provides no mechanism for how this occurs. In reality, cultural replication requires the actions of people, tools, and larger systems. This dependency is ignored in the meme analogy.
3. Over-Simplification of Culture  
   Culture is a complex, multi-level system involving individuals, groups, institutions, tools, and environments. Reducing this richness to a simplistic "meme" model ignores the systemic nature of cultural evolution and adaptation.
4. Holistic Dependence  
   Fractal HOLON/partons, as described by Ev Cult, rely on integration within larger systems to function. For example, a joke (a unit of culture) gets transmitted because people intentionally share it within specific contexts, not because it "self-replicates."
