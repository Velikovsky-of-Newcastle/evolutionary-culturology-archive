Introduction
Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult) unifies all domains of knowledge under a single meta-meta-scientific framework. 
 
At its core lies the concept of fractal HOLON/partons, which serve as the complete whole units of all systems, spanning from the quantum to the cosmic and from the individual to the cultural. 
 
These units operate under three universal laws:
1.
 
Integration upwards: Fractal HOLON/partons integrate into larger systems on the scale level above them.
2.
 
Cooperation/competition laterally: Units cooperate with or compete against others on the same scale.
3.
 
Command/control downwards: Each unit governs its subcomponents on the scale below.
 
Ev Cult applies these laws universally, bridging traditional disciplinary divides such as those between the natural sciences and the humanities.
 
For example, it unites the study of quantum physics with literary analysis by demonstrating how both domains follow the same principles of integration, cooperation/competition, and command/control. By doing so, Ev Cult provides a holistic understanding of reality that allows researchers to apply insights from one domain to challenges in another, fostering interdisciplinary innovation.

Theoretical Perspective
Fractal HOLON/partons: Universal Structures of Reality
Fractal HOLON/partons exist at every scale level, from atoms to galaxies, and from books to cultural movements. 
 
They are described as 'fractal' because their structural and functional patterns repeat across different scales (sizes), maintaining self-similarity, regardless of size or domain. 
This fractal property is fundamental to their universality, as it allows the same principles to govern systems ranging from the microscopic to the macroscopic and from the physical to the cultural. 
Each unit behaves as a whole system, part of a larger system, and a structure containing smaller subsystems. 
For example:
•
 
In physics, an atom integrates into molecules, interacts with other atoms, and governs subatomic particles.
•
 
In biology, an organism integrates into ecosystems, competes with other organisms, and commands its internal organs and tissues.
•
 
In culture, a book integrates into literary genres, competes with other books, and organizes its own chapters and paragraphs (of course, via a larger system, namely an author, and/or editors, publishers, etc).
These examples reveal the universality of the Three Laws, demonstrating their applicability across all 50,000+ domains.
Cultural Units: Transmission, Not Replication
“Memetics”, which conceptualizes cultural units (memes) as "self-replicating," fails to account for the role of larger systems in transmitting cultural units. This view is problematic because it overlooks the importance of systemic dynamics, where cultural units depend on interactions within and between larger systems (e.g., individuals, institutions) for their propagation. The meta-science of Systems Science emphasizes that replication is not autonomous, but requires mechanisms of communication, adaptation, and integration within these broader contexts. 
Ev Cult instead emphasizes that ideas, processes, and products—as cultural fractal HOLON/partons—are communicated by larger systems (e.g., individuals, institutions) through deliberate or unconscious transmission. 
This perspective aligns with Systems Science, recognizing the selective pressures acting on cultural units:
1.
 
Integration into larger systems (e.g., a scientific theory adopted by a community of researchers).
2.
 
Cooperation or competition within the same scale (e.g., competing ideologies or artistic movements).
3.
 
Command and control of subcomponents (e.g., organizing principles within an ideology).
As a meta-meta-science, Ev Cult applies to everything.
 
It thus solves: consilience, the unity of knowledge (see: E O Wilson’s 1998 book of the same name.)
