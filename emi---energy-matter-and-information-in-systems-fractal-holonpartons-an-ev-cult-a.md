Components of Systems, in Ev Cult: The Five Essential
Features
In Evolutionary Culturology, systems are characterized by
five core components or features, which define their structure and operation. 
These components are as follows:
1. 
Inputs:
 The resources,
materials, or information that enter the system. Inputs can be energy sources,
raw materials, data, or any other necessary resource that the system requires
to function.
2. 
Outputs: 
The products,
results, or tools that the system produces or utilizes. Outputs can also
include byproducts, waste, or information that the system generates and
releases back into its environment.
3. 
Algorithm/Processor: 
The
internal mechanism or process that drives the system. This can be an algorithm,
a set of rules, or a processing unit that governs how the system converts
inputs into outputs. The processor itself can have multiple subsystems, each
performing specific functions within the larger system.
4. 
System Boundary: 
The defined
limits or borders of the system. This boundary distinguishes the system from
its external environment and regulates the flow of inputs and outputs. The
boundary can be physical, such as the walls of a cell, or abstract, such as the
conceptual boundaries of a social system.
5. 
Wider Ecosystem/Environment:
The larger context or environment in which the system operates. This includes
the external systems, influences, and conditions that affect the system's
operation. The ecosystem can be natural, like the environment in which a
biological organism lives, or artificial, like the market in which an economic
system functions.

components, it can be classified as a system.

electronic.

traffic systems). 
Similarly, a cell (a biological system) has inputs

(cell membrane), and an environment (the organism's body).

as introduced in his paper 
Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for
Links

universe—"its"—are fundamentally derived from binary choices, or bits
of information. 
This idea suggests that the physical world is ultimately
constructed from informational processes, positioning information as the
fundamental building block of reality.
Relevance to Evolutionary Culturology
Wheeler's "it from bit" concept aligns closely
with the principles of the meta-meta-science of Evolutionary Culturology (Ev
Cult), particularly the emphasis on information as a crucial component of all
systems (fractal HOLON/partons).

five essential features: inputs, outputs, an internal processor or algorithm, a
system boundary, and a wider ecosystem or environment. Understanding these
features through Wheeler's lens deepens the perspective that information is not
merely a component of systems, but a foundational element from which matter and
energy emerge.
