A Brief Plot Summary of the novel `Middlemarch'
Middlemarch: A Study of Provincial Life 
is a novel by George Eliot, first published in 1871-1872. The narrative revolves around the fictional English town of Middlemarch, focusing on the interconnected lives of its inhabitants. It predominantly follows the stories of Dorothea Brooke, an idealistic young woman, and Tertius Lydgate, an ambitious young doctor. Both characters grapple with the constraints of society, ambition, and marriage, with their fates emblematic of the social and moral issues plaguing the early 19th-century provincial life.
The Genre, Sub-Genres, and Sub-Sub-Genres of `Middlemarch'
The novel is primarily considered to belong to the genre of realism, with a focus on psychological and social realism. It also explores sub-genres like the social novel and the 
bildungsroman
, and can also be classified under sub-sub-genres such as 
domestic fiction 
and 
marriage plot 
narratives.
Historical Context, and Similar Works
"Middlemarch" was written in a period marked by significant social and political change, including the Industrial Revolution, the agitation for democratic reform, and shifts in religious and scientific thought, including the publication of 
The Descent of Man 
(Charles Darwin, 1871). 
Its narrative is influenced by the historical conditions and reformist ideas of the time. Similar novels that explore the nuances of society and human psychology include Jane Austen's "Pride and Prejudice," Thomas Hardy's "Tess of the d'Urbervilles," and Henry James's "The Portrait of a Lady."
A Brief Biography of George Eliot
George Eliot, the pen name for Mary Ann Evans, was born on November 22, 1819, in Warwickshire, England. She was a leading novelist of the Victorian era, known for her psychological insight into character and society. Eliot wanted to escape the stereotype of women's writing being limited to lighthearted romances, hence the male pen name. Her other works include "Adam Bede," "The Mill on the Floss," and "Silas Marner." She died on December 22, 1880.
