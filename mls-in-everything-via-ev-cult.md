Genotype, Phenotype, & Trait
A 
genotype
 is the genetic code that defines an organism, while the 
phenotype 
is the physical manifestation of that code. 
Traits 
are 
specific characteristics 
that result from the interaction between genotype and environment. For example, the color of a butterfly's wings is a 
trait 
influenced by both its genetic makeup and the surroundings in which it develops.
Selection - and Deselection - in Evolution
Selection
 is the process by which certain traits become more common in a population due to their advantages in survival and reproduction. 
Deselection
, on the other hand, leads to the decline of less advantageous traits. 
This dynamic interplay shapes the diversity and complexity of life.
Types of Selection:
Natural Selection
: The survival of the fittest 
unit/group
 (i.e., the least-worst-adapted unit/group, to the environment/ecosystem), where nature favors traits that enhance survival.

A brief selection of some other types of Selection:
 
Artificial Selection
: Human intervention in breeding, such as in dog breeding.
Sexual Selection
: Traits that are attractive to the opposite sex, like the peacock's tail.
Unconscious Selection
: Unintentional human influence, like the domestication of wild plants.
Ecological Selection
: Environmental conditions favor certain phenotypes, such as cacti in arid deserts.
Stabilizing Selection
: Genetic diversity decreases as a population stabilizes on a trait, like human birth-weight.
Directional Selection
: Where an extreme phenotype is favored, like the long neck of the giraffe.
Disruptive Selection
: Selects against average individuals, like the contrasting beak sizes in finches.
Balancing Selection
: Maintains diversity, like the sickle-cell trait in malaria-prone areas.
Soft Selection
: Reduces variance without changing the mean, like height in humans.
Hard Selection
: Changes both mean and variance, like the development of antibiotic resistance in bacteria.
 
...(etc.)
"Selected-for" and "Selected-against" 
The terms "selected-for" and "selected-against" refer to traits that are favored - or disfavored - by selection. For example, thick fur is selected-for in cold climates, while it is selected-against in hot environments.
