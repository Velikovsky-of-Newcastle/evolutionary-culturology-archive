Abstract
This paper explores the concept of symbolic systems within various cultural domains through the lens of the meta-meta-science of Evolutionary Culturology. The Systems Model of Creativity
Csikszentmihalyi (2014) shows that creativity is a systemic phenomenon occurring at the intersection of (1) individual talent, (2) symbolic domains, and (3) social fields. 
He notes:
`
An Outline of the Systems Approach
 
Thus, starting from a strictly individual perspective on creativity, I was forced by facts to adopt a view that encompasses the environment in which the individual operates. This environment has two salient aspects: A cultural, or symbolic aspect which here is called the domain; and a social aspect called the field. 
Creativity is a process that can be observed only at the intersection where individuals [aka `agents'], domains, and fields interact...
...The domain is a necessary component of creativity because it is impossible to be a genius, at least by the definition used here, in the absence of a symbolic system. 
Original thought does not exist in a vacuum. It must operate on a set of rules, of representations, of notations [a symbolic system]. 
One can be a creative carpenter, cook, composer, chemist, or clergyman because the domains of woodworking, gastronomy, music, chemistry, and religion exist and one can evaluate performance by reference to their traditions. Without rules there cannot be exceptions, and without tradition there cannot be novelty. Creativity occurs when a person makes a change in a domain, a change that will be transmitted through time.'

Analysis of Symbolic Systems
Symbolic systems serve as the backbone for creativity in various domains. 
Creativity is the emergence of 
new, useful, and surprising 
units of culture (ideas, processes, products).
These systems provide the framework within which individuals/agents operate and innovate. 
For instance, in mathematics, symbolic systems such as equations and notations allow for the communication and development of complex ideas. Similarly, in the arts, symbols like musical notation and visual arts symbols enable artists to create and share their works.
Symbolic systems are crucial in maintaining the integrity of a domain. They facilitate the transmission of knowledge, and the evaluation of creative efforts against established standards. 
As Csikszentmihalyi notes, "Without rules there cannot be exceptions, and without tradition there cannot be novelty." This underscores the importance of symbolic systems in fostering both the preservation and evolution of cultural domains.
