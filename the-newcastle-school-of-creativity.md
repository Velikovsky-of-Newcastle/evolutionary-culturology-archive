The Newcastle School of Creativity: 
A Systematic Inquiry into Creative Production
Introduction
The 
Newcastle School of Creativity
 is an informal yet highly influential research collective comprising approximately thirty scholars dedicated to investigating creativity and the creative industries. Established in 2004, this group has made significant contributions to the study of creative processes through the integration of multiple theoretical frameworks, particularly those grounded in 
Csikszentmihalyi’s Systems Model of Creativity
, 
Bourdieu’s practice theory
, and 
structuration theory
 (Giddens, Archer, et al.).
Members of the Newcastle School with the School's mentor, Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi 
Theoretical Foundations and Core Research Themes
The Newcastle School’s research is deeply embedded in systemic perspectives on creativity. 
The group’s approach 
utilizes interdisciplinary methodologies to explore the structural and functional mechanisms of cultural production. 
Their research is influenced by several foundational frameworks:
Csikszentmihalyi’s Bio-Psycho-Socio-Cultural Systems Model of Creativity
 – Examines creativity as an emergent property of interactions between individuals, their domain of expertise, and the field that evaluates and legitimizes their work.
Bourdieu’s Field Theory
 – Provides a sociological lens for analyzing the creative process, particularly the power dynamics within cultural production systems.
Structuration Theory (Giddens, Archer, et al.)
 – Investigates how creative agents navigate and shape the structures in which they operate.

In his foreword to 
Educating for Creativity within Higher Education
 (2018), 
Distinguished Professor Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi
 praised The Newcastle School, stating:
“This group of researchers at Newcastle is at the forefront of scholarship dedicated to understanding how humankind has been using creativity in the past, and how the process can be supported in the future. To my knowledge, there has been no previous center anywhere on the planet where such a close-knit and dedicated group of scholars has existed quite like this one... What makes Newcastle different is that the scholars there are focusing on the understanding of creativity itself, instead of using their creativity to advance a particular discipline or domain. This process of self-reflection is indeed unique and becoming a real necessity for planning and directing the future.”
 
(Csikszentmihalyi, in McIntyre et al., 2018, p. xii)

Institutional Legacy: CAMR and CCIRP Research Groups
Between 2013 and 2020, the Newcastle School facilitated the 
Communication & Media Research Group (CAMR)
, a research initiative dedicated to advancing media and communication studies.
A Wayback Machine 
archival version of the CAMR Group website
The Newcastle School’s impact extends into the 
Creativity & Creative Industries Research Program (CCIRP)
, which continues to explore creativity in interdisciplinary contexts. As of 2022, CCIRP members included 
Dr. Phillip McIntyre, Dr. Susan Kerrigan, Dr. Janet Fulton, Dr. Michael Meany, and Dr. Chloe Killen
, among others.
Conclusion
The Newcastle School of Creativity
 represents a significant advancement in the scientific study of creativity. By integrating 
Csikszentmihalyi’s Systems Model of Creativity
, 
Bourdieu’s Field Theory
, and 
Structuration Theory
, the school has established a robust, interdisciplinary framework for analyzing creative processes. 
Their research continues to inform 
Evolutionary Culturology
, contributing to a more comprehensive understanding of how cultural production operates as a complex, systemic phenomenon.
