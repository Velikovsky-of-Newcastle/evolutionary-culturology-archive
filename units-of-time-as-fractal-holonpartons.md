4. Literature Review
4.1 Physics and Time
Time has been conceptualized in physics as both absolute and relative. Newtonian time posits an immutable, uniform passage of time, while Einsteinian relativity demonstrates that time dilates under gravity and velocity changes. Quantum physics introduces Planck time as the smallest measurable unit, showing that time itself may have a discrete structure.
4.2 Biological and Evolutionary Time
In evolutionary biology, time is linked to mutation rates, species adaptation, and mass extinction events. Geological time, defined by epochs and eras, integrates planetary transformations into biospheric evolution, highlighting the fractal nature of time as a nested system.
4.3 Cultural and Technological Time
Historical time has been traditionally understood through linear progressions or cyclical patterns. Technological time accelerates exponentially, as seen in Moore's Law, where computational power doubles approximately every two years. This acceleration suggests that time operates on a compressible and expandable scale, dictated by cultural and technological evolution.
4.4 Cognitive Time Perception
Psychology and neuroscience show that time is perceived differently depending on cognitive processes, attention, and emotional states. AI and automation are reshaping human time perception by structuring time into algorithmic cycles and predictive models.
