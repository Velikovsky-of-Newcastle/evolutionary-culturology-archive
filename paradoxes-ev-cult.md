On Solving - or Resolving - Paradoxes
The general method for resolving paradoxes usually involves one or more of the following approaches:
1.
 
Clarification of Definitions and Assumptions: Many paradoxes arise from vague or contradictory definitions. Clarifying or redefining terms can resolve the apparent contradiction.
2.
 
Advances in Understanding or Theory: Some paradoxes are resolved by advances in scientific or mathematical theory, which provide new insights or tools for addressing the issue.
3.
 
Logical Restructuring: This involves examining the logical structure of the paradox to identify hidden assumptions or fallacies.
4.
 
Reframing the Problem: Sometimes, what seems like a paradox is a result of looking at a problem in the wrong way. Reframing the issue can dissolve the paradox.
5.
 
Accepting the Paradox: In some cases, especially in philosophy, paradoxes are accepted as inherent contradictions that highlight the limits of human understanding or the complexities of the world.
6.
 
Empirical Investigation: For some paradoxes, especially in the sciences, empirical evidence can provide a resolution.
