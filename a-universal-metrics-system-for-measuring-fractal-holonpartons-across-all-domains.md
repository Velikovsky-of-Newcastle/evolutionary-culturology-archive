Abstract
 
This paper presents a formal, universal metrics system for identifying, measuring, and comparing the structure and function of 
fractal HOLON/partons (i.e., units / systems) 
across all domains of knowledge, as defined by the meta-meta-scientific paradigm of Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult). Grounded in The 3 Laws of 
fractal HOLON/partons
, and drawing from both meta-scientific and domain-specific literatures, the Ev Cult system allows for unified, cross-domain comparison of units ranging from biological cells, to novels, and governments. Two tables are presented: (1) a set of ten universal metrics and their theoretical grounding (Table 1); and (2) an illustrative application of these metrics across three representative systems from biology, literature, and governance (Table 2). This work provides the foundation for a comprehensive, quantitative science of cultural and systemic evolution.
Keywords
Evolutionary Culturology, fractal HOLON/partons, systems theory, universal metrics, cultural evolution, complexity, resilience, information theory, interdisciplinarity, consilience
 
Introduction
 
The unity of knowledge — consilience (see the book, by: E O Wilson, 1998) — requires that all domains of science, culture, and systems be comparably understood through universal principles. 
The meta-meta-science of Evolutionary Culturology has defined the universal unit of structure and function as the 
fractal HOLON/parton.
 A quantitative framework for comparing such units across domains has also been formalized. 
This essay presents a brief summary of that framework: a universal metrics system for evaluating the structural and functional properties of 
fractal HOLON/partons 
(the units)
 
in any/every system and domain. 
By applying this system to cases from biology, literature, and sociology, we demonstrate the empirical viability of a universal evaluative toolkit.
Theoretical Perspective
 
Ev Cult is a meta-meta-science that subsumes and integrates the insights of all meta-sciences—such as Systems Science, Information Science, Communication Science, Creativity Science, and the science of Cultural Evolution. 
Each of these meta-sciences emerged to unify particular sets of domains, yet none resolve the total problem of consilience. Ev Cult addresses this by defining the universal unit of analysis—the 
fractal HOLON/parton
—and showing that it operates in all individual sciences: mathematics, geometry, quantum physics, classical physics, chemistry, astronomy, geology, biology, psychology, sociology, anthropology, the arts/humanities, engineering, and design.
By framing all phenomena as structured and functional configurations of 
fractal HOLON/partons
, Ev Cult allows for formal comparisons across previously incommensurable systems.
Definitions
 
Fractal HOLON/partons: 
The universal unit of structure and function, simultaneously a whole and a part, embedded in a hierarchy of scale levels. 
Each one obeys the 3 Laws of fractal HOLON/partons.
Law #1 – Integration Upward: 
Each fractal HOLON/parton integrates into a larger supersystem on the scale level above. 
Law #2 – Cooperation/Competition Sideways: 
Each fractal HOLON/parton interacts laterally with other units at the same scale level through cooperation and/or competition. 
Law #3 – Command/Control Downwards/Inwards: 
Each fractal HOLON/parton exerts control over the smaller-scale units embedded within it.

On Unifying Frameworks, in Science and Culture
 
E.O. Wilson’s "Consilience" (1998), Koestler’s "holon" in the arts (1967), and Feynman’s "partons" in quantum physics (1975) each gesture toward universal structures of systems. 
Csikszentmihalyi’s Systems Model of Creativity (1988) articulates interactions between individual, domain, and field—each a nested system of cultural evolution. Shannon’s (1948) information theory and the advances in cultural evolutionary science by Boyd, Richerson, Mesoudi (2011), and Henrich demonstrate the explanatory power of universal units. 
However, while each of these contributions offers meta-scientific insight, none provide a unified quantitative system, applicable across all domains. This essay fills that gap.
