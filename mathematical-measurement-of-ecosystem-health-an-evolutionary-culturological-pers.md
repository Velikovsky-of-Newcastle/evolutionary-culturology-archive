0%: Represents a dead ecosystem, with no signs of life or functionality.

Data Resources for Implementation
The successful application of this method requires access to comprehensive and accurate ecological data. Below are some reputable sources where researchers can find datasets related to biodiversity, productivity, resilience, sustainability, and connectivity. These datasets can be plugged into the proposed model to assess Ecosystem Health Score (EHS%) across various levels:
Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF): Offers access to data about global biodiversity, including species occurrence records.
National Ecological Observatory Network (NEON): Provides open data that characterizes and quantifies ecological processes.
Ocean Biogeographic Information System (OBIS): A global open-access data repository for marine species distributions.
Forest Inventory and Analysis (FIA): Offers data related to forest health, growth, and inventory across the United States.
Long Term Ecological Research Network (LTER): Provides long-term datasets from diverse ecosystems across the United States.
European Environment Agency (EEA): Offers data and information on Europe's environment, including biodiversity and ecosystem assessments.
WorldClim: Global Climate Data: Provides global climate data for ecological modeling.
Conservation Science Partners (CSP): Offers datasets related to conservation and ecological analysis.
FAO Global Forest Resources Assessments (FRA): Provides extensive data on the world's forest resources.
The Global Lakes and Wetlands Database (GLWD): A comprehensive database for lakes and wetlands around the world.
