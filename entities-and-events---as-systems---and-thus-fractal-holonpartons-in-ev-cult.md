The Five (5) Key Components of a System, in Ev Cult
The systematic nature of entities and events can be elucidated through five critical components:
1. 
Inputs:
 Resources, energy, information, or materials that a system assimilates from its milieu. In biology, this includes nutrients for a cell; in events, it encompasses participants, and their contributions.
2. 
Outputs:
 Products or consequences emanating from the system's processes. Outputs can range from biological waste of an organism to the societal transformations following a political event.
3. 
Boundary:
 The demarcation separating the system from external elements. This can be a physical boundary like a cell membrane -- or a temporal-spatial boundary defining an event.
4. 
Internal Algorithms/Processors:
 Governing rules and mechanisms of a system. These might be biological pathways in living organisms - or procedural guidelines in events.
5. 
Ecosystem/Environment:
 The overarching context in which a system operates and interacts. For 
entities
, this may be an ecological or cultural environment; for 
events
, it encompasses the wider societal, historical, or environmental scenario.
 
Entities and Events as Systems
Entities as Systems:
 Entities, from a solitary cell - to a multifaceted organism - or societal institution - epitomize systemic characteristics. They maintain distinct boundaries, engage in internal processes, interact with their environments, and yield outcomes that impact both their surroundings and themselves.
Events as Systems:
 Events, be they as fleeting as a chemical reaction - or as extended as a historical movement - align with the systemic model. They originate from specific inputs, are driven by internal mechanisms, are defined spatially and temporally, result in discernible outputs, and transpire within a broader context.
