Ontogenesis in Different Domains, via Ev Cult
Biological Ontogenesis
In biology, ontogenesis refers to the development of an individual organism from fertilization to maturity. 
According to Ev Cult, this process is a 
fractal HOLON/parton 
phenomenon:
- Integration Upwards: The organism integrates into larger biological systems such as ecosystems and biomes. Cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs form organ systems, all of which are part of the larger organism. For example, human embryonic development involves the integration of cells into complex structures that form the various organs and systems of the body.
- Lateral Cooperation and Competition: Cells and tissues cooperate to maintain homeostasis while also competing for resources and space within the organism. For instance, during immune responses, different cell types must work together to combat pathogens while competing for nutrients and signaling molecules.
- Downward Command and Control: Genes and molecular signals regulate cellular functions and development, controlling the smaller units within the larger organism. This includes processes like gene expression regulation during differentiation and development.
Ontogenesis in Physical Systems
In classical physics and chemistry, ontogenesis can be seen in the formation and development of physical entities such as atoms, molecules, and crystalline structures:
- Integration Upwards: Atoms combine to form molecules, which further aggregate into larger structures like crystals or macromolecules. For example, the formation of a DNA double helix from individual nucleotides involves the integration of smaller units into a complex structure.
- Lateral Cooperation and Competition: Atoms and molecules interact through chemical bonds and forces, cooperating to form stable structures and competing for electrons and bonding sites. The interactions between different elements in a chemical reaction demonstrate this principle.
- Downward Command and Control: Subatomic particles (protons, neutrons, electrons) govern the behavior of atoms, while atomic structures dictate the properties and interactions of molecules. Quantum mechanics provides a framework for understanding these control mechanisms.
Ontogenesis in Astronomical Systems
In astronomy, ontogenesis is evident in the formation and evolution of celestial bodies and systems:
- Integration Upwards: Dust and gas clouds coalesce to form stars, which group into solar systems, galaxies, and eventually the universe. The process of star formation from nebulae is a clear example of integration upwards.
- Lateral Cooperation and Competition: Gravitational forces lead to the formation of planets and moons, which interact within solar systems, while galaxies compete for space and resources in the universe. The dynamics of galaxy clusters illustrate these interactions.
- Downward Command and Control: Fundamental forces and laws of physics control the behavior and development of stars and planets, influencing the larger structures they form. For instance, the lifecycle of a star is determined by nuclear fusion processes governed by physical laws.
Ontogenesis in Geological Systems
Geological ontogenesis involves the formation and transformation of Earth's features, such as rocks, minerals, and landscapes:
- Integration Upwards: Minerals combine to form rocks, which constitute larger geological features like mountains and continents. The rock cycle demonstrates the integration of different types of rocks into the Earth's crust.
- Lateral Cooperation and Competition: Tectonic plates and geological processes interact, creating and reshaping Earth's surface. Earthquakes and volcanic activity result from the competition and cooperation between tectonic plates.
- Downward Command and Control: The atomic and molecular structure of minerals dictates the properties of rocks, influencing larger geological formations. The crystalline structure of minerals controls their physical properties and behavior in geological processes.
Ontogenesis in Technological Systems
Technological ontogenesis refers to the development and evolution of technological artifacts and systems:
- Integration Upwards: Components integrate to form devices, which integrate into larger technological systems and networks. For instance, individual electronic components form circuits, which combine to create computers and other devices.
- Lateral Cooperation and Competition: Technologies interact and compete in the marketplace, influencing their adoption and evolution. The competition between different software platforms exemplifies this principle.
- Downward Command and Control: Design principles and technical standards govern the behavior and development of technological systems. For example, software development is controlled by programming languages and coding standards.
Ontogenesis in Cultural Systems
In the cultural domain, ontogenesis refers to the development of cultural artifacts (ideas, processes, and products) such as literature, art, and social institutions:
- Integration Upwards: Words form sentences, sentences form paragraphs, and paragraphs form books. Similarly, simple ideas combine to create complex ideologies and cultural systems. For example, the development of a novel involves the integration of individual chapters into a cohesive story.
- Lateral Cooperation and Competition: Cultural elements interact, blend, and compete for acceptance and dominance within societies. The spread of different art movements illustrates lateral cooperation and competition.
- Downward Command and Control: Foundational cultural units (e.g., symbols, language) influence the development of larger cultural constructs, guiding the creation and evolution of cultural artifacts. Linguistic structures control the way languages evolve and are used in communication.
