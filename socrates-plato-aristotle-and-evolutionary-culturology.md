Socrates, and Ev Cult

Aristophanes and Xenophon also wrote about Socrates, providing additional perspectives.

Plato, and Ev Cult
The corpus of Plato's works is not definitively fixed, as there has been debate over the authenticity of some texts attributed to him. However, the traditional corpus consists of approximately 36 dialogues, and 13 letters. It is important to note that some of these dialogues and letters are considered spurious by scholars.
What's up, with Plato?
If we count only the works generally accepted as authentic, the number is around 25 to 30 dialogues. Additionally, there are a number of works sometimes included in Plato's corpus that are now considered to be of doubtful authenticity.
To consider this within the framework of Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult), each dialogue and letter could be viewed as a 
fractal HOLON/parton 
within the larger intellectual and cultural milieu in which Plato was writing. These works have (1) 'integrated upwards' into the broader canon of Western philosophy and (2) 'co-operate laterally' with other philosophical works and ideas, all the while (3) exerting a 'downward' (command & control) influence on subsequent thinkers, and domains of study.

Aristotle, and Ev Cult
Aristotle's contributions to various domains of Science, Philosophy, and the Arts were extensive, but the precise number of works he authored is not definitively known. Many of his works have been lost over time, and what we have today is thought to represent only a fraction of his original output. 
What's up, with Aristotle?
The works attributed to him are generally categorized into several groups:
Logical works, collectively known as the "Organon."
Works on natural philosophy, including "Physics," "Metaphysics," "On the Heavens," among others.
Works on ethics and politics, such as "Nicomachean Ethics," "Eudemian Ethics," and "Politics."
Biological works, like "History of Animals," "Parts of Animals," and more.
Rhetorical works, including "Rhetoric" and "Poetics."
Various other works and fragments, some of which are of uncertain attribution.
A commonly referenced list, called the 
"Corpus Aristotelicum"
 identifies around 200 titles, but this includes works likely written by other authors or students of the Aristotelian tradition. Of these, approximately 31 works are generally accepted as genuine, but even within these, there are ongoing debates about the level of later editing, or interpolation.
