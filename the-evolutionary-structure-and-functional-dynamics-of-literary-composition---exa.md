3. Rates of Change (Evolution), Across Scale-levels
The rate of change varies significantly across the ten hierarchical scale-levels of literary composition. Patterns emerge that reveal how certain levels evolve rapidly, driven by dynamic selection pressures, while others exhibit remarkable stability due to their foundational role.
•
 
Scale-level 1: Literary Genres
o
 
Genres evolve relatively quickly, with new genres emerging every decade (e.g., LitRPG, Climate Fiction). The creation of hybrid genres accelerates change, driven by societal trends and technological innovations.
o
 
Selection Pressures: Cultural demand for relevance, adaptation to new media, and competition (Law #2) among genres ensure high evolutionary rates.
•
 
Scale-level 2: Novels
o
 
While novels evolve slower than genres, significant shifts in format (e.g., serialized web novels) and thematic focus reflect broader cultural changes.
o
 
Selection Pressures: Market demands and integration (Law #1) into evolving genres drive moderate rates of change.
•
 
Scale-levels 3–6: Chapters, Paragraphs, Sentences, Clauses
o
 
Mid-scale-levels experience slower, stylistic evolution. Trends like minimalist prose in modern fiction illustrate gradual changes rather than abrupt shifts.
o
 
Selection Pressures: Reader preferences for clarity and immediacy influence gradual stylistic adjustments, with competition (Law #2) and command/control (Law #3) maintaining coherence within works.
•
 
Scale-levels 7–10: Phrases, Words, Morphemes, Letters
o
 
Foundational levels exhibit the slowest rates of change. Words evolve with moderate regularity (e.g., "selfie"), while morphemes and letters remain highly stable.
o
 
Selection Pressures: Linguistic efficiency, technological constraints, and phonetic coherence ensure stability, though competition (Law #2) occasionally results in the adoption of new symbols (e.g., "@").

1.
 
Higher Evolutionary Rates: Observed at upper levels (genres, novels) due to societal and technological selection pressures.

4. Simultaneous Upwards and Downwards Causation
Literary composition exemplifies a fascinating dynamic of simultaneous upwards and downwards causation, where units across hierarchical scale-levels influence one another in bidirectional ways. 
This recursive interaction highlights the profound interconnectedness of fractal HOLON/partons:
•
 
Downwards Causation: When an author conceives an overarching idea for a novel, this high-level vision integrates (Law #1) into literary genres and commands and controls (Law #3) lower-level structures like plot, chapter organization, paragraph arrangement, and word choices.
Example: A dystopian novel’s thematic focus on environmental collapse dictates the use of somber tones and specific terminology across chapters and sentences.
•
 
Upwards Causation: As the author writes, emergent lower-level ideas can compete (Law #2) and shape higher-level decisions. A compelling line of dialogue, for instance, might influence a character’s traits, alter the trajectory of the plot, or even redefine the genre boundaries.
Example: An unplanned exchange between characters could inspire a subplot or even evolve the story into a hybrid genre like Climate Fiction with a romantic twist.

Conversely, environmental pressures at the ecosystem level dictate survival, reproduction, and molecular adaptations (downwards causation).

Astronomy: 
Solar systems demonstrate upwards causation when planetary dynamics, like asteroid impacts, can alter orbital configurations and influence the behavior of the system as a whole. Conversely, downwards causation occurs as the gravitational forces of stars shape planetary orbits and surface conditions.

Engineering:
 In mechanical systems, the design of individual components (e.g., gears or circuits) can influence the performance of the entire machine (upwards causation). Conversely, the machine’s intended function dictates the design, material selection, and arrangement of its components (downwards causation). This interplay ensures both functionality and innovation in engineering systems.
