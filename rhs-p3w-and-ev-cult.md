Abstract
This paper presents a comparative analysis between (1) the
theoretical framework proposed in "Relational Holon Science and Popper's 3
Worlds in Engineering Practice" by David Blockley, et al. (2022), and (2) the
principles of Evolutionary Culturology (Ev Cult). While the former focuses on
systems engineering through the lens of relational holon science and Popper's
metaphysical ontology, the latter offers a broader, more universal framework
for understanding cultural evolution across all domains. This study explores
the intersections and divergences between these two approaches, highlighting
how they each contribute to our understanding of complex systems in their domains.

Relational Holon Science, Evolutionary Culturology, Popper's
Three Worlds, Systems Engineering, Cultural Evolution, Holarchies, Causality

Literature Review
Summary of "Relational Holon Science and Popper's 3
Worlds in Engineering Practice" (2022)
The paper "Relational Holon Science and Popper's 3
Worlds in Engineering Practice" by David Blockley, et al. (2022), integrates
Rosen's relational biology, Popper's Three Worlds theory, and relational holon
science to create a theoretical foundation for addressing complex engineering
challenges. By conceptualizing systems as holons—units that are simultaneously
wholes and parts of larger systems—the authors provide a holistic approach to
managing the interconnectedness and causality within engineering systems.
The paper emphasizes the importance of holarchy, the layered
structure of holons, and the closure of causal loops, which are crucial for
maintaining system identity and integrity. Popper's Three Worlds theory is
extended to link the material reality (World 1), subjective experience (World
2), and formal knowledge (World 3), thereby creating a framework for relating
formal and natural systems. The practical application of these concepts is
highlighted through methodologies such as Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) and
Observe-Orient-Decide-Act (OODA), which align with the cyclic nature of the
four causes.
Ultimately, the paper positions relational holon science as
a critical tool for engineers to navigate and manage the complexity and
unpredictability inherent in modern engineering projects, by fostering a
holistic understanding of systems that integrates both human and natural
factors.

The theoretical foundations of "Relational Holon

2022) are rooted in systems engineering, drawing heavily on Rosen's relational

framework is particularly concerned with the practical application of these
theories in engineering projects, emphasizing the importance of holarchies,
causal loops, and anticipatory systems.

 that encompasses all

Comparative Analysis
Holarchies and System Structure
Both "Relational Holon Science and Popper's 3 Worlds in
Engineering Practice" (2022) and Ev Cult (2016-2024) utilize the concept of holarchies (or in Ev Cult, 
fractal HOLARCHY/partarchies
) to
explain the layered structure of systems. 
In Blockley et al.'s (2022) framework, holarchies
are primarily applied to engineering and biological systems, where they help
manage the complexity and dimensionality of these systems. Ev Cult, however,
extends the concept of 
fractal HOLARCHY/partarchies 
universally, applying it to all domains to understand the structure and evolution of all units.
The broader application of 
fractal HOLARCHY/partarchies
 in Ev Cult allows for
a more comprehensive analysis of cultural systems, where 
units of culture (ideas, processes, products) 
are
understood as 
fractal HOLON/partons 
that interact dynamically across different
scales. 
This approach provides a more holistic view of cultural evolution,
encompassing not only the structural aspects of systems, but also their dynamic
processes.
Causality and Anticipation
Causality plays a central role in both frameworks, though it
is interpreted differently in each. In "Relational Holon Science and
Popper's 3 Worlds in Engineering Practice," causality is rooted in
Aristotle's four causes, with a focus on the closure of causal loops and the
anticipatory nature of systems in engineering. This approach is particularly
concerned with ensuring the integrity and functionality of engineered systems, through a rigorous application of causal principles.
Ev Cult also recognizes the importance of causality, but it
incorporates a broader range of causal influences that emerge from the
evolutionary dynamics of culture. 
In Ev Cult, anticipation is viewed as one
aspect of a larger process of cultural evolution, where cultural units adapt
and evolve over time. This dynamic view of causality reflects the
interdisciplinary nature of Ev Cult, which integrates insights from all Domains - ranging from physics to biology, from sociology to the arts, to create a more comprehensive understanding of physical, chemical, cosmic/astronomical, geological, biological, psychological, sociological, anthropological and cultural evolution.
Integration of Formal and Natural Systems
A key difference between the two frameworks lies in their
approach to formal and natural systems. Blockley et al. maintain a distinction
between formal systems (models) and natural systems (actual systems), using
formal systems to manage and understand natural systems in engineering
practice. This distinction is critical for the practical application of
relational holon science, in managing complex engineering projects.
Ev Cult, on the other hand, integrates formal and natural
systems into a single taxonomy of all culture (information, energy, and matter). 
This integration reflects the
universal scope of Ev Cult, where cultural units are understood as both formal
and 
natural entities, that evolve over time. By unifying formal and natural
systems, Ev Cult provides a more holistic framework for analyzing the evolution
of culture across all domains.
Discussion
The comparative analysis of "Relational Holon Science
and Popper's 3 Worlds in Engineering Practice" and Evolutionary
Culturology reveals significant overlaps in their use of holarchies (or in Ev Cult's case: 
fractal HOLARCHY/partarchies
), causality,
and anticipatory systems.
However, while Blockley et al.'s framework is
specifically tailored to the needs of Systems Engineering, Ev Cult offers a
broader, more universal application across all domains.
The differences between these frameworks highlight their
distinct purposes and scopes. Blockley et al. focus on the practical
application of holon theory in engineering, emphasizing the need for rigorous
causal analysis and system integrity. Ev Cult, by contrast, adopts a more
dynamic and evolutionary perspective, viewing 
systems 
and 
units of culture
 as continuously
evolving entities, that interact (via 3 Laws) across different scales and domains.
