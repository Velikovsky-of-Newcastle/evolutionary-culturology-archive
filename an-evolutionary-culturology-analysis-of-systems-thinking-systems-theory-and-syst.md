The term 'fractal HOLON/partons' refers to the universal units of all systems and cultural inputs and outputs, embodying the principles of integration, cooperation/competition, and command/control across scales. This framework underpins the analysis of 'systems thinking,' 'systems theory,' and 'systems science' within the Ev Cult model.
Ev Cult also posits that all cultural units - including scientific ideas - evolve through a 7-phase lifecycle. 
These 7 phases are derived from biological and cultural evolutionary processes:
1.
 
Emergence: Early formation of ideas or nascent conceptual phases (e.g., Wave 1 in Elliott Waves; pre-paradigmatic phase in Kuhn’s model).
 
 
2.
 
Adoption/Selection: Competitive selection among ideas for adoption or rejection (e.g., innovators and early adopters in Diffusion of Innovation).
 
3.
 
Expansion/Growth: Rapid growth and widespread adoption (e.g., Wave 3 in Elliott Waves; early majority in the Hype Cycle).
 
4.
 
Peak Maturity: Institutionalization and full acceptance (e.g., normal science in Kuhn).
 
5.
 
Stabilization: Plateau of activity, with diminishing dynamism (e.g., the Hype Cycle's Plateau of Productivity).
 
6.
 
Decline/Fragmentation: Decreasing relevance or fragmentation into subfields (e.g., Wave 5 in Elliott Waves; cultural fixation).
 
7.
 
Reintegration/Transformation: Core ideas reabsorbed into new paradigms, initiating fresh cycles (e.g., creative destruction in systems innovation).
 
These 7 phases also serve as a universal template for interpreting the trajectories of ideas, as demonstrated through the 27 lenses applied to the Ngram data. (See Table 1.)

Comparison of the 27 Frameworks
Each of the 27 frameworks analyzed in the table offers a unique, but comparable, perspective on the lifecycle of concepts. 
Below is a very brief explanation of the 27 frameworks:
1.
 
Elliott Waves (Elliott, 1938):
 
(1) Wave 1: Early emergence of the trend.
(2) Wave 2: Initial corrections or consolidation.
(3) Wave 3: Rapid adoption and widespread growth.
(4) Wave 4: Plateau or minor decline.
(5) Wave 5: Peak followed by decline or fragmentation.
 
Elliott Waves provide an insightful lens for interpreting the Ngram data. For instance, the decline of 'systems theory' aligns with Wave 5, signaling a plateau and fragmentation phase. Meanwhile, 'systems thinking,' currently in Wave 3, illustrates rapid adoption and integration into diverse domains, indicative of an expansive growth phase.
 
2.
 
Kuhn’s Paradigms (1962) (Kuhn, 1962):
(1) Pre-paradigmatic exploration: Competing ideas form.
(2) Paradigm formation: Dominant framework solidifies.
(3) Normal science: Paradigm becomes institutionalized.
(4) Anomalies arise: Challenges to the dominant paradigm.
(5) Paradigm shift: Revolutionary change occurs.
 
3.
 
Diffusion of Innovation (Rogers, 1962):
 
o
 
(1) Innovators: Concept is introduced.
o
 
(2) Early adopters: Influential groups adopt the concept.
o
 
(3) Early majority: Widespread social adoption begins.
o
 
(4) Late majority: Adoption peaks, becoming the norm.
o
 
(5) Laggards: Slow adopters follow.
 
4.
 
Social Constructionism (Berger & Luckmann, 1966):
 
o
 
(1) Initial social creation: Concepts emerge from discourse.
o
 
(2) Social adoption: Ideas gain legitimacy within groups.
o
 
(3) Cultural integration: Concepts become widely accepted.
o
 
(4) Institutionalization: Concepts are embedded in norms.
o
 
(5) Decline: Concepts lose relevance as society evolves.
5.
 
Diffusion of Responsibility (Darley & Latané, 1968):
o
 
(1) Awareness: Shared responsibility is recognized.
o
 
(2) Adoption: Groups begin sharing responsibility.
o
 
(3) Expansion: The behavior becomes widespread.
o
 
(4) Stabilization: Responsibility-sharing normalizes.
o
 
(5) Fragmentation: Responsibility diffuses or diminishes.
6.
 
Historical Periodization (Various, 1969):
o
 
(1) Preceding era: Sets the foundation.
o
 
(2) Emergence: Intellectual trends develop.
o
 
(3) Growth: Era peaks with widespread influence.
o
 
(4) Stabilization: Trends become established norms.
o
 
(5) Decline: Transition to the next era.
7.
 
Systems Thinking Evolutionary Lens (Laszlo; Checkland, 1972, 1981):
o
 
(1) Conceptual emergence: Systems thinking is defined.
o
 
(2) Adoption: The approach is applied across domains.
o
 
(3) Growth: Hard and soft systems methodologies expand.
o
 
(4) Stabilization: Systems thinking becomes interdisciplinary.
o
 
(5) Specialization: Fields develop specific applications.
8.
 
Evolutionary Epistemology (Campbell; Popper, 1974, 1972):
o
 
(1) Variation: Emergence of new ideas.
o
 
(2) Selection: Ideas are tested and validated.
o
 
(3) Retention: Successful ideas are preserved.
o
 
(4) Expansion: Ideas integrate into broader knowledge.
o
 
(5) Reorganization: New cycles of thought emerge.
9.
 
Critical Realism (Bhaskar, 1975):
o
 
(1) Empirical: Observation of patterns.
o
 
(2) Actual: Identification of causal processes.
o
 
(3) Real: Understanding underlying structures.
10.
 
Knowledge Diffusion and Memetics (Dawkins, 1976):
•
 
(1) Creation: "Memes" (units of culture) are generated.
•
 
(2) Social adoption: Memes are selectively embraced.
•
 
(3) Spread: Memes become viral.
•
 
(4) Stabilization: Memes integrate into culture.
•
 
(5) Mutation or extinction: Memes evolve or fade.
11.
 
Lifecycle Stages (Adizes, 1979):
•
 
(1) Birth: Concept formation.
•
 
(2) Growth: Rapid expansion and adoption.
•
 
(3) Maturity: Peak institutionalization.
•
 
(4) Decline: Decrease in relevance.
•
 
(5) Death: Concept fades or is replaced.
12.
 
Social Network Analysis (Freeman, 1979):
•
 
(1) Network formation: Connections are established.
•
 
(2) Influencers emerge: Key hubs drive adoption.
•
 
(3) Growth: Networks expand.
•
 
(4) Stabilization: Connections solidify.
•
 
(5) Fragmentation: Networks lose cohesion.
13.
 
Hierarchy Theory (Allen & Starr, 1982):
•
 
(1) Local dynamics: Interaction within subsystems.
•
 
(2) Adoption: Systems integrate into larger hierarchies.
•
 
(3) Stabilization: Hierarchical systems solidify.
14.
 
Fractal Geometry (Mandelbrot, 1982):
•
 
Not a staged model; instead, describes scalability and patterns.
15.
 
Cultural Evolutionary Dynamics (Boyd & Richerson, 1985):
•
 
(1) Variation: Ideas emerge.
•
 
(2) Selection: Beneficial ideas are adopted.
•
 
(3) Retention: Ideas persist through culture.

(1) Exploitation (r): Growth phase.
•
 
(2) Conservation (K): Stability and accumulation.
•
 
(3) Release (Ω): Collapse or disturbance.

(4) Reorganization (α): Innovation and renewal.

Institutionalization of Knowledge (Latour, 1987):

(1) Emergence of ideas.

(2) Adoption by institutions.

(3) Stabilization through practices.

(3) Growth of knowledge networks.

(4) Stabilization of systems.

(5) Fragmentation or decline.

(3) Growth of complexity modeling.

(4) Stabilization in academia.

(5) Refinement or fragmentation.

(4) Stabilization post-shift.

(4) Stabilization.

Evolutionary Developmental Biology (Evo-Devo) (Carroll, 2005):

(1) Emergence of developmental processes.

(2) Growth and adaptation.

(3) Maturity and optimization.

(4) Stabilization.

(5) Transformation through evolution.

Innovation Ecosystem Models (Adner, 2006):

(1) Ecosystem formation.
