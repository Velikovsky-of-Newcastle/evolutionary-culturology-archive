Q: What is, a gamut-?
A: In medieval music, the term "gamut" refers to the full range of musical notes, that were available for use in Gregorian chant, and other forms of early Western music.
 
The concept of the gamut is closely associated with the work of Guido of Arezzo, an 11th-century music theorist and monk who developed a system for teaching sight-singing and music theory.
Historical Context
Guido of Arezzo: Guido's contributions to music theory include the development of the hexachord system and the introduction of solmization syllables (ut, re, mi, fa, sol, la), which are the ancestors of the modern solfège system (do, re, mi, etc.).
Hexachord System: Guido's hexachord system was based on a series of six-note patterns (C-D-E-F-G-A) that could be transposed to start on different pitches (G, C, and F).
The Gamut
Definition: The term "gamut" originally referred to the entire scale of musical notes that could be produced within Guido's hexachord system. Range: The full range of the medieval gamut spanned from the note G at the bottom of the bass clef to the note E at the top of the treble clef, encompassing three overlapping hexachords.
Musical Notation: Guido's system also included a method of notation that allowed singers to learn melodies more easily by associating pitches with specific hand gestures, known as the "Guidonian hand."
