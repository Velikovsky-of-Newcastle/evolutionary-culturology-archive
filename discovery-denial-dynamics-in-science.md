The Three Stages of Scientific Discovery:

Abstract
Scientific discoveries often undergo a predictable sequence
of societal reactions: (1) initial denial, (2) subsequent dismissal of significance,
and (3) eventual misattribution of credit. This paper examines this phenomenon
within the framework of the meta-meta-science of Evolutionary Culturology, using examples from various
scientific domains. By analyzing these stages, we gain insights into the
cultural dynamics that shape scientific progress and recognition. This
understanding is essential for fostering a more accurate and equitable acknowledgment
of scientific contributions.

Introduction
The process of scientific discovery is not merely a journey
from ignorance to knowledge, but a complex cultural phenomenon - characterized by 3 distinct stages of societal response. 
A quote from Bill Bryson's "A Short History of Everything" (2004):
"Alexander Von Humboldt... may have had Agassiz at least party in mind when he observed that 
there are three stages in scientific discovery: first, people deny that it is true; then they deny that it is important; finally they credit the wrong person.
" (Bryson 2004, p. 508) 
Alexander Von Humboldt's observation
encapsulates this process: (1) discoveries are first denied, (2) then deemed
unimportant, and finally (3) credited to the wrong person. 
This paper explores
these stages through the lens of Evolutionary Culturology, highlighting the
interplay of cultural and scientific dynamics.
