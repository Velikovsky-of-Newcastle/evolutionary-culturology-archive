Literature Review
Auguste Comte's Work
Auguste Comte (born: 1798; died: 1857), a prominent French philosopher, is best known for founding the discipline of Sociology, and developing the doctrine of 
positivism
. 
His major works, which have had a significant impact on the domain of Sociology and beyond, include:
- "Plan of the Scientific Operations Necessary for the Reorganization of Society" (1822): Co-authored with Henri de Saint-Simon, this early work laid out Comte's vision for a scientific approach to reorganizing society.
- "Course of Positive Philosophy" (1830–1842): This six-volume work is Comte's magnum opus, where he established the epistemological foundation of positivism and outlined the hierarchy of the sciences. It was later translated and condensed by Harriet Martineau, in 1853.
- "A Discourse on the Positive Spirit" (1844): Initially an introduction to a work on astronomy, this book was republished as a standalone piece by the Paris Positivist Society. It was later translated into English by Edward Spencer Beesly, in 1903.
- "A General View of Positivism" (1848): This work provides an overview of Comte's approach to social issues, through the lens of positivism. It was translated into English by J.H. Bridges, in 1865.
- "System of Positive Polity" (1851–1854): This four-volume series further develops Comte's ideas on Sociology, emphasizing the importance of morality, and the need for a political system that supports moral progress. It also introduces the concept of the "Religion of Humanity."
These works collectively form the cornerstone of Comte's contributions to Sociology, and the philosophy of science, underpinning the development of modern social science methodologies, and theories.
